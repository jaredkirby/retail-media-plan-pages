Our shared
challenges.

Help clients make effective retail media investments. Deliver them reliably. Understand what they accomplish.

Our work is to keep business objectives, approved plans, actual execution, spending, and expected results aligned as conditions change, and to recognize when the plan itself needs to change.

02 / Our shared challenges

One direction. Room to go deeper.

Each statement defines a challenge we need to solve. It does not assume that every challenge is currently a failure. Open the detail to see the specific questions underneath.

01

Turning business goals into a credible, agreed plan

Clients have business goals, limited budgets, and expectations. Those do not automatically translate into a clear advertising plan. We need to determine what is worth doing, what it can reasonably accomplish, and what everyone is agreeing to.

We are succeeding whenThe client and team can explain what we are doing, why we are doing it, what we expect, and what we have committed to deliver.
Explore this challenge
1.1

Choosing where to invest

Translate business priorities into choices about retailers, products, audiences, tactics, timing, and budget. Account for distribution, product economics, promotions, competition, and retailer commitments.

Which investments deserve funding, and what tradeoffs are we making?

1.2

Estimating achievable results

Use relevant history and stated assumptions to estimate an outcome range. Recognize uncertainty and compare previous forecasts with actual results.

What can this investment reasonably produce, and what could change that estimate?

1.3

Establishing a shared agreement

Agree on objectives, success measures, scope, budgets, timing, responsibilities, and dependencies. Discuss any gap between the client's target and the forecast before activation.

Does everyone understand the same plan and the same commitments?

02

Keeping actual execution aligned with the approved plan

An approved plan can diverge from reality. Launches slip, products become unavailable, settings are incorrect, budgets change, and canceled activity can remain live. We need to prevent these gaps where possible and catch them quickly when they occur.

We are succeeding whenWe can verify that actual activity matches the latest approved plan. Every material difference has an explanation, an owner, and a next step.
Explore this challenge
2.1

Getting programs ready and running correctly

Confirm product availability, product pages, creative, approvals, access, and measurement readiness. Verify products, targeting, bids, budgets, dates, and tracking at launch and during delivery.

Is the approved activity actually running, and can shoppers buy what we advertise?

2.2

Keeping spending under control

Reconcile approved, committed, spent, invoiced, and remaining amounts. Compare platform settings with planning records and forecast final spend.

Where will spending finish, and which differences need correction or approval?

2.3

Carrying changes through completely

Carry reallocations, delays, cancellations, and other approved changes through campaigns, planning records, forecasts, billing, and communications. Preserve the approval history.

Has the change reached every affected place, and have we verified it?

03

Knowing what is happening and what the investment is achieving

Data is spread across systems, arrives at different times, and does not always mean the same thing. Even accurate reporting can leave us uncertain about why results changed or whether advertising produced additional business.

We are succeeding whenThe team and client understand the current situation, the likely outcome, and the limits of what the evidence can tell us.
Explore this challenge
3.1

Establishing a trustworthy view

Connect approvals, execution, spending, and results. Check completeness, freshness, metric definitions, attribution windows, and reporting lag. Make missing or immature data visible.

Can people trust this view and tell which comparisons are valid?

3.2

Interpreting results correctly

Compare actual results with expectations. Investigate delivery, availability, pricing, competition, media performance, and measurement as possible explanations. Distinguish attributed outcomes from estimates of incremental impact.

What changed, what probably explains it, and what remains unknown?

04

Turning information into timely decisions and completed action

Knowing about a problem does not resolve it. Updates, findings, and recommendations can stall when the right person does not receive them, decision authority is unclear, or nobody follows through.

We are succeeding whenMaterial findings lead to a decision, a completed action, or an explicit reason to continue without changes. People know what they own without repeated chasing.
Explore this challenge
4.1

Getting attention and decisions at the right time

Prioritize issues by consequence and urgency. Explain what changed, why it matters, the recommended response, and who can approve it. Use regular updates and immediate escalation when needed.

Who needs to know or decide, and by when?

4.2

Following work through to resolution

Assign actions, deadlines, and completion conditions. Make supported changes to bids, budgets, products, creative, targeting, or the broader plan. Verify implementation and review the effect at an appropriate time.

Was the action completed correctly, and did it produce the intended result?

05

Making good work repeatable and sustainable

Reliable service becomes difficult when it depends on individual memory, repeated manual effort, or more work than the team can deliver. Experience also loses value if each program starts from scratch.

We are succeeding whenQuality remains consistent as workloads change, people take time off, and accounts grow. We improve through experience and deliver the service at a viable margin.
Explore this challenge
5.1

Reducing dependence on individual people

Make account history, approvals, recurring checks, and working methods accessible. Reduce avoidable manual reconciliation and establish coverage for essential responsibilities.

Can someone else understand the account and continue the work?

5.2

Learning across programs

Preserve what worked, what failed, and under what conditions. Record tests, decisions, forecast errors, and outcomes so they improve future choices.

What will we do differently next time because of what we learned?

5.3

Matching commitments to capacity

Match service scope to staffing, skills, tools, time, and fees. Identify scope growth, rework, and unnecessary complexity before they undermine delivery.

Can we deliver the agreed service consistently with the resources available?

03 / Shared definitions

Make the agreement precise.

A goal, a prediction, and a promise serve different purposes. Keep them separate so an ambition does not quietly become a commitment.

Objective

The business outcome we want to influence.

Target

The level of achievement we are pursuing.

Forecast

What we expect under stated assumptions.

Commitment

What we have explicitly agreed to deliver.

Example: acquiring new households is an objective. The target defines the ambition. The forecast estimates what the budget can produce. Commitments define the agreed work and any explicit guarantees.

Keep the original expectation visible.

When a launch slips or the budget changes, update the outlook and record why. Preserve these four views together.

Original targetWhat we initially set out to achieve.
Latest approved planWhat we are currently authorized to execute.
Current forecastWhat we now expect, given actual conditions.
Actual resultsWhat has happened so far, with the reporting date and limitations.

Control and dependency

We may own campaign settings while relying on clients or retailers for inventory, pricing, content, and approvals. Each dependency needs an owner and a response when it threatens the plan.

Budget and performance

Spending the budget is not proof of success. Underspending may reflect a delivery failure, limited opportunity, or a justified decision to avoid waste. Explain which applies.

Attribution and incrementality

Attributed sales follow a reporting rule. Incremental sales estimate what advertising caused beyond what would otherwise have happened. State which the evidence supports.

Action and verified resolution

Finding an incorrect budget starts the work. Resolution means correcting it, verifying the change, updating affected records, and informing the relevant people.

04 / Keeping the team aligned

Six questions for the weekly update.

Use the same questions each week so changes, decisions, and unfinished work remain visible.

  1. What changed in the objectives, approved plan, or assumptions?
  2. What is live, blocked, delayed, or incorrect?
  3. Where do we expect spending to finish, and what needs adjustment?
  4. What do the available results tell us, what do we now expect, and what remains uncertain?
  5. What actions are underway, and what have we learned from previous actions?
  6. What decisions are needed, from whom, and by when?

Material problems need communication when discovered. The weekly update tracks their progress and resolution.

05 / Working with the framework

Define the problem before the solution.

Keep the five shared challenges stable. Add specific issues underneath them as we learn. Give each issue a primary home and note any related challenges.

Example · Challenge 02 / Spending control

“We need a dashboard” proposes a solution. The underlying problem might be:

We cannot reliably identify which programs are likely to overspend without manually checking several systems. That delays corrections and makes budget control dependent on whoever performs the check.

A simple structure for the next issue

What is happening?Describe the observable gap or recurring difficulty.
Why does it matter?Explain the effect on the client, the work, or the agency.
What evidence do we have?Use examples, frequency, time lost, or financial impact.
What would better look like?Define an outcome we can recognize or measure.
Who owns the next step?Name an owner, a deadline, and any required decision.
How will we verify progress?Agree on the evidence that would show improvement.

This framework is a shared direction for investigation and improvement. It is not a claim that every problem needs a new tool or a separate project.
